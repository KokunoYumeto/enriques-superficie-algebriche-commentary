# Method and limits

## Scope and conventions

This continuation concerns Federigo Enriques, *Le superficie algebriche* (1949), Capitoli X–XI, and completion of the commentary on Capitoli VII–XI. The principal source is the photographic scan. The transcription is a searchable guide, not an independent authority for a doubtful sign or letter. An editorial note marked “Stampa,” “Dubbio,” or “RISOLTO” is a prior assessment to be checked; it is not evidence that the image has already been verified in this continuation.

The complete X–XI transcription was read, including unnumbered assertions, displayed calculations, footnotes, historical notes, and the closing classification tables. The chapter reviews distinguish the truth of a result from the completeness of its printed argument. A correct theorem with an omitted construction remains classified as a sketch where appropriate; a modern replacement proof does not turn the historical proof into a complete one. Conversely, a false intermediate claim does not invalidate a conclusion for which a separate proof is supplied.

All modern statements concern smooth connected projective surfaces over the complex numbers unless another hypothesis is explicitly stated. The notation is $p_g=h^0(K_X)$, $q=h^1(\mathcal O_X)$, $p_a=p_g-q=\chi(\mathcal O_X)-1$, and $P_m=h^0(mK_X)$. On a minimal nonruled surface the absolute linear genus is $p^{(1)}=K_X^2+1$. Relative linear genus, the genus of a pencil's base, and the genus of its members are distinguished. In particular, Enriques's “fascio ellittico” usually specifies an elliptic base, whereas “fascio di curve ellittiche” specifies genus-one fibres. The historical word “iperellittica” can mean an abelian surface; it does not always mean a modern bielliptic surface.

## Claim and correction records

The claim table is organized in source order. A row records a definition, proposition, lemma, formula, intermediate inference, construction assertion, or mathematically material historical assertion. Closely linked identities in one calculation may be stated together when separating them would obscure their dependence. Independent conclusions, hypotheses, and defects receive separate treatment. Historical attributions are distinguished from independently established priority claims. The five permitted values of `kind` classify the presentation in the book: `definition`, `proved`, `sketched`, `stated without proof`, and `assumed from earlier work`.

The correction table has a different purpose. Its “transcribed reading” and “scan reading” fields record what the edition gives and what is visible, while the decision and evidence distinguish a transcription error from a slip or misprint already in the book. A mathematically proposed replacement is stated in the evidence; it is not silently substituted into the diplomatic scan-reading field. The approximate line field identifies the passage and, for an uncertainty marker, the exact line of the attached X–XI transcription. Those line numbers refer to the source file, not to the completed commentary.

Every `% ??` marker is treated individually, including marks already accompanied by an editorial resolution. Some concern more than one glyph in a single marked passage; the corresponding record addresses each component. Magnification resolves many indices and punctuation marks, but it does not create information absent from the photographic image. Where ink damage and a scanning speck remain visually inseparable, the record gives the adopted reading, the evidence, and the residual uncertainty. “Resolved” in those cases means that an explicit editorial disposition replaces an unattended question mark; it does not certify the physical origin of every speck. Mathematical corrections supported by adjunction, ramification, or a consistency calculation are separately identified as such.

## Mathematical verification

The principal checks use adjunction, Riemann–Roch and Serre duality, the Hodge index theorem, Noether's formula, the Euler characteristic of a fibration, Riemann–Hurwitz, finite étale descent, invariant differential forms, and the canonical bundle formula. Each use retains the assumptions that make it valid: minimality or relative minimality, connected fibres, the distinction between arithmetic and geometric genus, exact torsion order, independence of Kummer characters, and the difference between numerical and linear equivalence. Dimension estimates are not treated as proofs of effectivity, irreducibility, a prescribed degeneration, or the existence of a global family.

The most consequential Chapter X check is the free diagonal quotient with $p_g=0$, $q=1$, $K^2=0$, Kodaira dimension one, and no global vector field. Its covering curves, freeness, Hodge numbers, multiple fibres, and plurigenus formula are calculated explicitly. It disproves the asserted exhaustive elliptic-group description, while leaving the ruledness criterion to be proved separately. The corrected proof of that criterion includes the omitted nontranslation monodromy case. The radical normal forms are checked using valuations at every branch point, including infinity; the incorrect degree-eight variants have genus three rather than one. The small plurigenus tables are checked from the floor formula and the abelian monodromy constraint, not by interpolating the printed entries.

For Chapter XI the review separates the classification by Kodaira dimension from the stronger and false characterization by continuous automorphism groups. It examines the Albanese and Picard arguments, the scope of abelian-surface polarization claims, the low-invariant regularity assertions, and the two inequalities in the p. 459 footnote. Modern theorems are used with their stated scope, including the correction to Debarre's argument in his addendum. Neither a parameter count nor the existence of a linear system is identified with a proof that its map is birational.

## Earlier chapters and earlier assessments

The commentary on Capitoli VII–IX was read and revised as part of completing Part 2. The source and the earlier audit files were consulted at the passages needed to validate its corrections, examples, references, and hypotheses. This is a targeted reconsideration of those earlier chapters, not a new claim-by-claim audit of all pp. 1–370. The original Part 1 fragment is preserved unchanged. Its notation, relevant inherited hypotheses, label conventions, and reference-list style were consulted where the continuation depends on them.

The section “Corrections to earlier audits” in `commentary.md` identifies the exact earlier claim IDs and explains what changes. It distinguishes a wrong earlier assessment from a correct earlier calculation repeated in the checkpoint, and from an erroneous proposal in the edition's apparatus that the earlier audit had already rejected. In particular, the p. 349 proposal to replace $C_8$ by $C_5$ is not misrepresented as a newly discovered error of the earlier audit.

The book commentary states the mathematics in its own right. Audit IDs, confidence assessments, review history, and the account of how verification was performed belong to the accompanying records. The removal of process language does not remove the underlying examples, repairs, qualifications, or counterexamples.

The review distinguishes three levels of evidence. A scan establishes the visible print to the limits of its resolution. An algebraic calculation can force the intended mathematical correction without establishing the physical shape of a damaged glyph. A modern theorem can establish the result under its hypotheses without proving that the historical argument already contained that proof. None of those levels substitutes for another.

## Exact calculations and consistency controls

The arithmetic checks supplement, rather than replace, the written proofs. They used exact rational coefficients, finite permutations, and integer floor/ceiling calculations:

- Both p. 378 Riemann–Roch expressions were compared coefficient by coefficient with the projective-dimension bound.
- The eight-sheet and nine-sheet branch permutations were checked for their generated group orders, commutativity, and product-one relation.
- The inertia orders at every finite branch value and at infinity were used to recompute the genera of the correct and incorrect harmonic models, the cubic-radical model, the two cyclic numerical counterexamples, and the Fermat-quartic example.
- All 16 harmonic and 30 equianharmonic composition entries were checked; exactly one equianharmonic entry is erroneous.
- The seven canonical periods were compared with the floor formula for every integer from 1 through 48.
- The closed formulas for the third, fourth, and sixth plurigenera were cross-checked, and the twelfth-plurigenus case analysis was checked on all ordered triples with indices from 2 through 80. This finite search neither proves the unbounded theorem nor establishes geometric realizability from the least-common-multiple condition.
- The coefficient of the fourth power of the transverse coordinate in the eliminated ordinary quartic was recomputed.

The output schemas, allowed categories, source-line marker crosswalk, identifier preservation, and commentary-label references were checked separately. A supplemental error on the same line as an uncertainty marker is not counted as a second resolution. Conversely, two separate uncertainty markers on one printed page remain separate entries.

This exposed an additional error inherited from the checkpoint: the inadmissible signature $(2,4,8)$ has ceiling sum $6+3+2=11$, so the formal formula gives $P_{12}=2$. Its exclusion is the abelian least-common-multiple obstruction, not a failure of the numerical strict bound. X249, X254, the relevant correction evidence, and the book commentary now agree. The separate formal signature $(2,5,5)$ correctly gives $P_{12}=1$.

The earlier-audit correction section contains fifteen entries: twelve substantive or reading corrections, and three locator/date corrections. Some physical readings remain limited by the available image. The completed work does not claim a new classification of all marked radical-cover data, a reconstruction of the unavailable private communication, or an independent reproduction of every historical memoir. Suggestions for extending the calculations are explicitly labelled as this review's proposals with confidence and reasons. They are not represented as established new theorems or as problems known to remain open in current mathematics.
