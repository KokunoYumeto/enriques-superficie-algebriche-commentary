# Audit record: Capitoli X–XI

This record accompanies the claim table, the diplomatic correction table, and the completed commentary on Capitoli VII–XI. Page references are to Enriques’s printed pagination. Section identifiers beginning `cp2-` are stable labels in `commentary_part2.tex`. “High confidence” indicates a direct calculation, an explicit counterexample, or a source-checked theorem with matching hypotheses; “moderate confidence” marks an editorial or geometric inference with a stated limitation. Confidence is not a probability estimate or a claim of historical priority.

The core distinction is between a result, its printed proof, and a modern replacement proof. In particular, the failure of the asserted exhaustive description of surfaces with $p_g=0$, $q=1$ does not invalidate either the ruledness criterion or the seven-family bielliptic classification. The latter conclusions are proved separately with the omitted cases and corrected normal forms included.

## Capitolo X: main findings

1. **The negative-canonical-intersection criterion is valid; the classical argument is abbreviated (pp. 372–376; `cp2-x-2`).** For an integral curve, the genus in $C^2>2p_a(C)-2$ is arithmetic genus. An exceptional $(-1)$-curve must be excluded. On a minimal nonruled model the canonical divisor is nef, and pulling back from that model proves the single-curve statement. The book's alternative argument through terminal adjoints and continuous families needs effectivity, control of fixed components, independence of imposed point conditions, and a justification that the claimed boundary member exists. The isolated inequality $r\geq3\pi-5$ is not a general ruledness criterion: a genus-two K3 polarization already contradicts such a reading. The presence of infinitely many exceptional curves is a sufficient condition for ruledness; it does not assert that every ruled model has infinitely many exceptional curves. On p. 375, the dotted relative linear genus is visible in the first formula and footnote; the dot is lost in the later displayed inequality without a justified change of model.

2. **The local fibre-defect lemma is correct after its conventions are made explicit (pp. 376–378; `cp2-x-3`).** For a fibre $F$, reduced support $D$, and a smooth minimal nonruled surface, the uniform expression is $\delta(F)=K_X\cdot(F-D)-D^2+\sum\mu_x(D)$. This handles singular components without confusing arithmetic and normalization genera. Every summand is nonnegative. A degenerate fibre with zero defect is a multiple of a smooth elliptic curve; a reduced smooth fibre of higher genus also has zero defect and must not be accidentally excluded by the wording. The footnote on p. 377 prints $(t+1)(2\rho-2)$; its correct factor is $t-1$, although the error disappears numerically when $\rho=1$.

3. **There are two errors in the Riemann–Roch dimension estimate on p. 378, not just the marked sign error (`cp2-x-3`, claim X047).** With $D^2=i>0$ and $p_a(D)=\rho$, the eventual projective-dimension bound is $p_a(X)+r^2i/2-r(2\rho-2-i)/2$. The expanded expression has $+r$, not the printed $-r$, and no final $+1$. The extra $+1$ belongs to the bound for $h^0$, not $\dim|rD|$. The two printed expressions differ by $-2r$; the printed right side exceeds the actual Riemann–Roch dimension bound by one. This review identified the extra constant, and both defects are recorded. The contradiction only uses quadratic growth, so the mathematical conclusion survives.

4. **The ruledness conclusion for $p_g=0,q>1$ is sound (pp. 378–379; `cp2-x-4`).** Once the Albanese pencil and the local-defect lemma are available, Noether's formula gives an impossible negative total fibre defect if the general fibre has positive genus. The ruling therefore has base genus $q=-p_a$. This does not require the later, false exhaustion of the $q=1$ case.

5. **The Albanese-genus-greater-than-one case has smooth reduced fibres, but the original construction of Lemmas I–II contains real gaps (pp. 379–392; `cp2-x-5`–`cp2-x-8`).** If a multiple fibre is $F=mD$, the defect is $(m-1)(2g(D)-2)$, and $2\pi-2=m(2g(D)-2)$, where $\pi$ is the general-fibre genus. The checkpoint mistakenly used $\pi$ in the first factor's genus term; this is corrected. A continuous family of Picard twists has one extra parameter beyond the dimension of an individual complete linear system. The printed incidence dimension counts do not, by themselves, force a member containing the chosen fibre. Equality of numerical characters supplies a Riemann–Roch lower bound, not equality of actual dimensions.

6. **Torsion-class descent and rational-divisor selection must be separated (pp. 381–392; `cp2-x-6`–`cp2-x-8`).** A finite étale base change can make a torsion class monodromy invariant. A rationally given complete linear series may still be a nonsplit Brauer–Severi variety over a general field. Over $\mathbb{C}(B)$, Tsen's theorem makes the Brauer obstruction vanish; the Stacks Project Tags 03RD and 03RF are the exact modern references already used in the commentary. The cover preserves $K^2=0$ and $\chi=0$, not the individual invariants $p_g$ and $q$. The final reference to Hypothesis II on p. 392 is a printed wrong label: the cover constructed there verifies Hypothesis I. The covariant constructions with pencils of quadrics and special contact divisors do not establish all needed restriction-map, rationality, and degeneration properties. Their class arithmetic is retained; the missing geometric existence steps are identified.

7. **Non-equivalence does not force a new reduced component (pp. 387–389; `cp2-x-7`, claim X103).** The original product example is sound for linear nonequivalence: $3F_p+F_q$ and $F_p+3F_q$, with $p-q$ of order four, have identical support and a nonzero two-torsion difference, but remain paraequivalent. This review retains that example and adds a stronger one. Let $X=(H\times E')/\langle(\iota,T_a)\rangle$, where $H$ has genus two, $\iota$ is its hyperelliptic involution, and $a\ne0$ is two-torsion on $E'$. This quotient has $p_g=0,q=1$ and smooth genus-two Albanese fibres. Its other fibration has six double fibres $2E_i\sim F$, with $2K_X\sim2F$. The divisors $C=2E_1+2E_2\in|2K_X|$ and $D=3E_1+E_2$ have the same support and the required degree/genus, yet $D-C$ restricts to the nonzero two-torsion class $P_1-P_2$ on $H$. It is therefore not in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$. This refutes the precise non-paraequivalence-to-new-support inference in the numerical setting of the lemma. It does not refute Lemma II's existential conclusion. Norm maps may also kill the relevant torsion or alter coefficients, so the final pushdown step needs its own proof.

8. **Lemma III and the second elliptic pencil are valid in the genus-greater-than-one setting (pp. 392–394; `cp2-x-9`–`cp2-x-10`).** The difference of the two divisor classes is vertical. Intersecting with the horizontal parapluricanonical divisor forces the base class to have degree zero, giving the claimed paraequivalence and square zero. For the second-fibration theorem, a complete modern replacement is obtained from isotriviality over the elliptic base: the period map lifts to a bounded domain and is constant, Torelli fixes the fibre, and a finite étale base change gives a product. The quotient group acts by translations on the elliptic factor and has rational quotient on the higher-genus factor. The translation action descends to the required elliptic pencil over $\mathbb{P}^1$. The historical ruled-model action-lifting argument alone does not supply every descent step.

9. **The extension to genus-one Albanese fibres is false (pp. 394–398; `cp2-x-11`).** Isotriviality does not imply translation monodromy. The printed branch-crossing argument incorrectly adds minimal transposition lengths; products can cancel. It also conflates a singular pulled-back curve with a singular total surface. The explicit checkpoint quotient remains valid after verification: take a double cover of an elliptic curve branched at two points, pull it back along an étale double cover, and let $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ act diagonally with one nonzero translation and one inversion on another elliptic curve. The action is free, $p_g=0,q=1,K^2=0,\kappa=1$, and the Albanese fibration has two double smooth fibres. There is no nonzero vector field, and the other pencil has genus three. This disproves the universal second-elliptic-pencil assertion and its later exhaustive summaries. Catanese et al. (2025), Lemma 1.1(2) and Appendix B, provide the matching modern nontranslation framework.

10. **Both paraelliptic examples on p. 397 need careful correction (`cp2-x-11`).** In the double-cover example with four horizontal and six vertical branch components, the correct values are $p_g=3,p_a=2$, not the printed $4,3$. The half-branch divisor on $B\times\mathbb{P}^1$ has degrees $(3,2)$; its 24 crossing singularities are rational double points. In the triple-cover example, three triple contacts plus one double and one simple contact total 12, impossible for two cubics. Either reduce the number of triple contacts to two, or replace the second cubic by a quartic. The residual branch weights then give the stated $p_g=1,p_a=0$. The spelling `contratto` is a separate print error. The suggested numerical division between elliptic and paraelliptic surfaces is not exhaustive: the nontranslation counterexample already has $p_a=-1$.

11. **The translation-quotient description is useful after its scope is restricted (pp. 398–405; `cp2-x-12`–`cp2-x-13`).** For $(C\times E)/G$, with faithful translations on $E$, one obtains $q=g(C/G)+1$, $p_g=g(C/G)$, and $\chi=0$. The determinant refers to the chosen pair of connected pencils. The sign in the p. 400 Zeuthen–Segre identity must be plus: $13+12p_a-p^{(1)}$. A finite translation subgroup of an elliptic curve has at most two generators; an arbitrary finite automorphism group need not. The product example $u^2=P_4(z)\psi^2$ correctly illustrates why disconnected fibre equations lower the determinant and produce $p_g=1$. For a cyclic étale Kummer cover, the contact class must have exact order $n$, not merely be nonzero. For two radicals, their character classes must be independent. Branch exponents must include the valuation at infinity, and a connected cyclic transverse cover requires $\gcd(n,r_1,\ldots,r_t)=1$. The abelian local orders satisfy the necessary lcm condition because the commuting inertia elements have product one. The normal-form reduction of an ambient polynomial is properly a function-field operation at a fixed torsion class, not an arbitrary polynomial factorization.

12. **The plurigenus formula is correct, but several subsequent numerical assertions are not (pp. 406–412; `cp2-x-14`).** On the minimal translation quotient over $\mathbb{P}^1$, $K_X\sim-2F+\sum(s_i-1)F_i$, and

   \[
   P_m=\max\{0,1+m(t-2)-\sum_i\lceil m/s_i\rceil\}.
   \]

   The pushforward proof explains both the integral residual coefficients and completeness of the linear system. On p. 406, the preliminary bound should be $0\leq k_i\leq s_i-1<s_i$; the stricter printed $k_i<s_i-1$ is not what the subtraction establishes. On p. 408 the ramification sum loses its factor $1/s_i$. The following weak inequalities are true but insufficient: they must be strengthened from $1$ to $2$ to obtain the ensuing $t=3$ estimate. The p. 409 case split must use $s_1=3$ in the third case if it is to be disjoint. Even with that repair, its constant values of $P_4,P_6$ are false. The exact formulas, with $a_j$ counting multiplicities equal to $j$, are $P_3=\max(0,1-a_2)$, $P_4=\max(0,2-a_2-a_3)$, and $P_6=\max(0,4-2a_2-a_3-a_4-a_5)$. Actual cyclic examples with signatures $(3,4,12)$ and $(2,5,10)$ refute the respective printed values. The Fermat-quartic signature $(4,4,4)$, paired with a faithful elliptic translation action of $(\mathbb{Z}/4)^2$, gives another actual counterexample to the fourth-case value.

13. **The strict $P_{12}$ conclusion survives, but the printed proof has several separate faults (pp. 410–412; `cp2-x-14`).** Restore the floor brackets in the p. 411 formula. For $t>4$, the sum is bounded by $6t$, not $24$, giving $P_{12}\geq6t-23>1$. The p. 412 table includes the impossible triple $(2,4,8)$, which violates the lcm condition. Its ceiling sum is $6+3+2=11$, so the numerical formula gives $P_{12}=2$, consistent with the strict plurigenus bound. Its purported $(3,3,s_3>3)$ subcase is also excluded by the lcm condition, and its last line must include $s_1=4$. The formal signature $(2,5,5)$ in the Geppert footnote correctly demonstrates why positive orbifold degree alone is not enough: it gives $P_{12}=1$ but is not realizable in this abelian translation setting. The repaired case analysis proves $P_{12}>1$ in the positive-degree translation class.

14. **The global ruledness criterion is true despite the failed classification step (pp. 405–412; `cp2-x-14`).** The missing genus-one Albanese case is handled by the canonical bundle formula over an elliptic base, keeping the degree-zero Hodge line and its finite multiplier character. Any multiple fibre makes $P_4>0$. Without multiple fibres, the canonical character has order $1,2,3,4$, or $6$, and the $p_g=0$ hypothesis excludes order one; therefore $P_4+P_6>0$. Thus ruledness is equivalent to $P_4=P_6=0$, and also to $P_{12}=0$. The general canonical-bundle reference is Bombieri–Mumford (1977), §1, Theorem 2, pp. 27–29; a $q=0$-specific source should not be substituted for this case. Within minimal $p_g=0,q=1$ surfaces, $P_{12}=1$ still characterizes the bielliptic case.

15. **The bielliptic group classification is sound after the rank and connectedness hypotheses are stated (pp. 414–424; `cp2-x-16`–`cp2-x-18`).** The seven groups are $\mathbb{Z}/2$, $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$, $\mathbb{Z}/4$, $\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4$, $\mathbb{Z}/3$, $\mathbb{Z}/6$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/3)^2$. Commuting translations are in the kernel of multiplier minus one; the group must also embed faithfully in translations on the other elliptic factor. The strict adjunction inequality on p. 415 needs transversality: a vertical elliptic fibre gives equality. The ordinary type $I_b$ radical equation is valid after $x-b\to z-b$, but the auxiliary claim that its eliminated plane equation is quadratic in $z$ is false generically: its $z^4$ coefficient is $(\varphi-\psi)^2$. Its generic two nodes are finite, not the asserted pair at and infinitely near infinity. The harmonic composition table has all 16 entries correct. The equianharmonic table has one wrong entry out of 30: row four, entry three, must be $\tau\omega=\pi$, not $\pi\omega=\pi$.

16. **The harmonic equations must be distinguished; the p. 419 formula is the correct one (pp. 418–421; `cp2-x-17`).** The degree-four exponent vectors $(1,1,2)$ and $(3,3,2)$ give the same unmarked harmonic elliptic curve over $\mathbb{C}$; inverse inertia markings do not establish birational distinction. The degree-eight p. 419 model has inertia $(4,4,2)$, no branch at infinity, and genus one. The alternative on p. 420 and the final $II_b$ equation on p. 421 each introduce a fourth branch value of order two at infinity, and each has genus three. Their exact branch letters and exponents are visible in the scans. Restore the p. 419 model for $II_b$. The resultant argument does not prove rationality of conjugate branches; the independent Kummer field and distinct conjugate sums give a valid primitive-element proof. Exact-order-four/six torsion is necessary for composite radical indices. For $III_c$, distinct nonzero three-torsion classes can be negatives, so the final condition must be linear independence over $\mathbb{F}_3$.

17. **The final plurigenus table and canonical cover are governed by the canonical character (pp. 424–426; `cp2-x-19`).** The canonical orders of the seven types are $2,2,4,4,3,6,3$. In every case $P_m=1$ exactly when the order divides $m$, and $P_m=0$ otherwise. Hence the printed $P_4=1$ is wrong for all three equianharmonic types, and the shared $P_3=0$ is additionally wrong for $III_c$. The modulus labels must refer to $K$, not the translation orbit $C$. The canonical cover is the connected finite étale cover associated to the exact torsion class of $K_X$; it is an abelian surface with $p_g=1,q=2$ and all plurigenera one. On p. 426 both subscripts read $2n-8$, but the surface is $F_m$, so the degree is $2m-8$. Taking a square root of an arbitrary ambient polynomial does not alone prove étaleness; the biadjoint construction must be interpreted via the canonical cover and normalization.

## Our examples and proposed extensions for Capitolo X

These are contributions or proposed continuations of this review, not assertions of first discovery in the literature.

- **A same-support counterexample to the exact paraequivalence inference — high confidence.** The genus-two quotient developed in this review and described above is stronger than the original product diagnostic. Its freeness, $p_g,q$, six double fibres, canonical class, divisor degrees, and nontrivial restriction $P_1-P_2$ have all been checked directly. This is a finished example, not a conjecture. It isolates the coefficient-versus-support mistake without disputing the existence of the second elliptic pencil.
- **A second actual counterexample to the coarse $P_6$ table — high confidence.** The Fermat quartic has a $(\mathbb{Z}/4)^2$ action with signature $(4,4,4)$; its quotient by that group is rational and the curve has genus three. Pairing the action with a faithful subgroup of four-torsion translations on an elliptic curve gives a free diagonal action and a surface in the relevant translation class. The floor formula gives $P_6=1$, contradicting the printed constant $2$. This extends the already sound cyclic examples in the checkpoint.
- **An exact arithmetic classifier for finite abelian cover data — medium confidence as a proposed project, high confidence in the necessary tests.** Given a finite abelian group and inertia vectors, check generation, the sum-zero relation, inertia orders, branch data at infinity, and the rank-two embedding required on the elliptic factor. Then compute genus and plurigenera. Such a classifier would separate actual covers from formal multiplicity lists and would systematically catch the $(2,4,8)$ and $(2,5,5)$ failures. The finite tests included here are verification examples, not a complete implementation or a new classification theorem.
- **A comparison of translation and nontranslation Hodge characters — high confidence as a useful organizing principle.** The canonical bundle formula over the elliptic Albanese base retains a degree-zero Hodge line. Tracking its character at the same time as multiple fibres explains both the failure of the chapter's exhaustive summary and the survival of the ruledness criterion. A systematic tabulation against the modern isotrivial quotient literature is a follow-up task; no claim is made that it is an open problem in current mathematics.
- **An invariant audit of the radical models — high confidence for the calculations already completed.** Infinity valuations, primitive elements, and canonical characters give compact independent tests for every explicit normal form. Extending these checks to marked versus unmarked cover equivalence would clarify what the book intends by “tipo simile.” The assertion that the printed inverse-generator elliptic curves are unmarked-birationally different is already disproved; a full classification of the associated marked surface data has not been attempted here.

## Capitolo XI: main findings

1. **The four numerical classes survive; the system and model conventions matter** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-1`, `cp2-xi-7`, `cp2-xi-9`.] (pp. 428–429, 455–458, 463–464; §§1, 7, 9). Adjunction gives `n−(2π−2)=−K·H`. The sign classifications concern suitable positive moving systems and the minimal canonical square. An elliptic fibre has equality on a Kodaira-dimension-one surface, so equality for an arbitrary divisor does not force torsion canonical class. The historical “infinitely many exceptional curves” cannot uniformly mean infinitely many exceptional curves of the first kind: minimal P² and P¹×P¹ contain no (−1)-curves. The final `P12` classification requires the effective plurigenus theorem, not just the definition of Kodaira dimension.

2. **The continuous-group ruledness criteria are valid, but several local arguments are incomplete** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-2`.] (pp. 429–433; §2). The centralizer does not necessarily fix every eigenpoint individually. Point conditions need not lower a stabilizer dimension independently. Fixed-degree birational maps form an algebraic locus, not generally a group under composition. Brion's properness result for the connected automorphism group of a nonuniruled projective variety replaces these reductions. On a minimal nonruled complex surface, Aut⁰ is an abelian variety; its action on a fixed projective linear system is trivial, and an effective action has finite stabilizers. The fourth criterion means unbounded dimensions of product families, not merely infinitely many discrete automorphism components.

3. **The numerical identities in the fourth criterion are correct** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-2`.] (p. 433; §2). Resolving the base cluster gives `N=n+Σh_i²`, `Π=π+Σh_i(h_i−1)/2`, and `N−2Π=n−2π+Σh_i`. The gap lies in proving that the image curves form an r-dimensional family, equivalently controlling the stabilizer of the chosen curve. The inequality `N≥r−1` follows from the characteristic normal system once its hypotheses hold. It is not necessary to alter the correctly printed algebra.

4. **Classification from an existing group is sound** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-3`.] (pp. 433–438; §3). Dimension two gives an abelian surface, whose transitive effective translation action is simply transitive. Dimension one in Kodaira dimension one gives a free quotient `(C×E)/G` with translation action on E, so `q=b+1`, `pg=b`, `χ=K²=0`. The bielliptic Kodaira-dimension-zero cases must also be retained. The Hurwitz formula on p. 435 is correct under its uniform-ramification assumptions; the letters C and K are interchanged in the printed list.

5. **The reciprocal group characterization is false because the canonical formula loses a degree-zero line bundle** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-4`, `cp2-xi-7`, `cp2-xi-8`, `cp2-xi-9`.] (pp. 438–443, repeated 458, 460–462, 464; §§4, 7–9). For a relatively minimal genus-one fibration in characteristic zero, the canonical formula includes `L=(R¹f_*O_X)^∨`, of degree χ. For χ=0, L can be nontrivial torsion. Then `q=b`, `pg=b−1`, whereas trivial L gives `q=b+1`, `pg=b`. Constant j and zero Euler defects do not imply trivial L. The reader-facing formula is now sourced to Bombieri–Mumford (1977), §1, Theorem 2, pp. 27–29. Their L is the dual of the line denoted L in the commentary; the notation conversion is explicit.

6. **An explicit smooth quotient disproves all relevant versions of that converse** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-monodromy-example`.] (pp. 438–443, 460–462, 464; §4, label `cp2-xi-monodromy-example`). Let C→B be a connected étale double cover and let σ be its free involution. The free diagonal quotient `(C×E)/⟨(σ,−1)⟩` has `K²=χ=0`, `q=g(B)`, `pg=g(B)−1`, and no vector fields. For genus B=2 it has `pg=1,q=2,P4=7` and Albanese image a genus-two curve. For genus B=3 it has `pg=2,q=3`, directly addressing the book's pg>1 and pg≠1 hypotheses. The latter base choice repairs two checkpoint examples whose first displayed numbers did not match the claim under discussion.

7. **Two further assertions were absent as distinct ledger entries** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-3`, `cp2-xi-4`.] (pp. 439 and 443; §§3–4). XI212 records the strict `π>ρ` in the transverse-pencil paragraph. The scan shows an ordinary strict sign; the determinant-one product has `π=ρ`, and Riemann–Hurwitz gives only the weak comparison in general. XI211 records the omission of pg=0 bielliptic surfaces from the forward summary's group list. This is independent of the false reverse implication already recorded in XI087. These additions account for 212 rows rather than the checkpoint's 210.

8. **The Albanese lemma survives its stated hypothesis; the next lemma does not** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-4`.] (pp. 440–442; §4). If q=2 and there is no pencil over a genus-two curve, the Albanese image is a surface. The erroneous argument then tries to exclude precisely that pencil. Its Euler coefficient 8 must be 4. Even after correction, one gets elliptic fibres and zero canonical square, not pg≥2. Nontrivial differential monodromy gives pg=1 and refutes the exclusion.

9. **The multiple-fibre plurigenus calculation is valid in its translation case** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-4`.] (pp. 442–443; §4). Over an elliptic base with trivial Hodge line, squaring the canonical divisor provides one moving whole fibre for each multiple fibre. Two or more such fibres give P2≥2; one gives P4≥2. The conclusion P4>1 distinguishes positive canonical order from the abelian case but does not imply a transformation group. The quotient counterexample has P4=7.

10. **The branch-curve numerical contradiction is conditional but arithmetically sound** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-4`.] (pp. 444–445; §4). From `24χ≥6(ρ−1)−2τ` and `2δ+3τ≤2(ρ+δ+τ−1)`, one obtains `τ≤2(ρ−1)` and χ>0 for ρ>1. The source itself acknowledges that this does not cover arbitrary multiple branch components. It cannot be used as a complete argument for all ramified covers without reduced nodal/cuspidal hypotheses and a justified moving characteristic system.

11. **The pg=1, K²=0 trichotomy overlaps and misses a base genus** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-5`.] (pp. 447–450; §5). K3 and abelian surfaces can themselves carry genus-one fibrations. A disjoint statement separates κ=0 from κ=1. The properly elliptic case can have a genus-two base when q=2 and the Hodge line is nontrivial. The statement q≤2 is nevertheless correct and follows from Castelnuovo–de Franchis and Euler nonnegativity. Proposition c is internally inconsistent: it assumes zero canonical order and then allows P4>1. Its intended literal emendation is not uniquely determined; the two mathematically valid alternatives are separated in the commentary.

12. **The three p. 449 defects require three repairs** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-5`.] (§5). The first comparison reverses; the second line needs both a reversed sign and an added final +1; and the equality case is pa=−1, not pa=1. The correct nonnegative expression is `e=12(pa+1)−p^(1)+1`, so `12(pa+1)≥p^(1)−1≥0`. The inherited inline annotation suggesting only reversal of the second inequality was insufficient.

13. **The torsion types and P12=1 criterion are correct with the usual minimal-model and plurigenus hypotheses** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-5`, `cp2-xi-7`.] (p. 450; §§5, 7). Canonical orders are 2 for Enriques, 2/3/4/6 for bielliptic, and 1 for K3/abelian. A nontrivial numerically trivial line bundle has no section, so `Pm=1` exactly when its torsion order divides m. The seventh printed type `III_e` is a mislabel for `III_c`. The distinction between polarized families and unpolarized isomorphism classes is retained.

14. **Polarization, isogeny, and moduli arguments must be separated** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-6`.] (pp. 451–454; §6). The divisor δ is attached to the chosen primitive polarization `(1,δ)`. A maximal isotropic subgroup gives a degree-δ isogeny to a principally polarized surface; Milne's exact descent locators are Part I, Proposition 13.8, Remark 13.9, and Corollary 13.10. The resulting surface is a genus-two Jacobian or a product with its product polarization (Voight, Theorem 43.5.1). Equal three-dimensional moduli counts do not prove dominance. The strict higher-genus moduli inequality only holds for g≥4. A nodal curve with the expected number of nodes requires existence and irreducibility, not just an expected-dimension count. A geometric-genus-two integral curve does have trivial translation stabilizer, by normalization and Riemann–Hurwitz.

15. **The genus-three illustration supplies an isogeny construction, not the asserted complete moduli argument** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-6`.] (p. 454; §6). Four simple branch points satisfy Hurwitz, but transitivity and the global monodromy relation must also hold. Counting `1+4−1=4` gives the fixed-monodromy Hurwitz dimension. Removing one elliptic modulus does not prove dominance of a Prym map. The complementary abelian-surface factor is not shown principally polarized by the chosen intersection quotient; an additional polarization isogeny can supply the Jacobian/product alternative.

16. **The historical quotient-classification statements remain attributed dependencies** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-6`.] (p. 455; §6). The page does not state the full group lists or hypotheses of Enriques–Severi, Bagnera–De Franchis, or Godeaux's work. It does not prove that every finite correspondence is Galois. The bibliography agent handles the recoverable primary metadata; the commentary retains these historical qualifications without saying how the present review was made.

17. **Fixed-invariant general-type boundedness has a complete modern repair** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-7`.] (pp. 458, 464; §7). Bombieri's 5K canonical-model theorem provides a uniform embedding of the canonical model. Its Hilbert polynomial is `χ+(25t²−5t)K²/2`. Fixing K² and χ gives a finite-type Hilbert scheme. Noether's inequality and χ≥1 leave only finitely many possible χ for fixed K². This gives finitely many bounded algebraic families, not finitely many surfaces and not a classification of all components.

18. **Both p. 459 irregular canonical inequalities are false under the printed hypothesis** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-7`, `cp2-xi-false-bound-q2`, `cp2-xi-false-bound-q3`.] (§7, labels `cp2-xi-false-bound-q2` and `cp2-xi-false-bound-q3`). A smooth double cover of `B₂×P¹` with branch line bundle L=(degree 4,O(3)) has `(χ,pg,q,K²)=(9,10,2,24)`, and canonical degree two onto an embedded surface, violating K²≥3χ. A smooth double cover of `B₂×E` with L of bidegrees (4,3) has `(χ,pg,q,K²)=(15,17,3,72)` and two-dimensional canonical image, violating K²≥6χ−3. Smoothness, ampleness, H¹(L⁻¹)=0, and canonical-image dimension were checked, not merely the numerical formulas. Debarre's valid general bound K²≥2pg must be cited with his 1983 Addendum, which completes the canonical-pencil case of the 1982 argument.

19. **Castelnuovo–de Franchis gives the correct irrational-pencil threshold** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-8`.] (pp. 459–462; §8). The projective wedge-kernel/Grassmannian dimension argument yields `pg≥2q−3` if there is no pencil with base genus at least two. Its contrapositive is exactly the book's `pg≥2pa+4`. The reader-facing proof includes closedness of one-forms, descent to the base, absence of poles, and descent across exceptional rational curves. For χ<0, the corrected Euler expression `e=Δ+4(g−1)(b−1)` forces ruledness. The omitted base term on pp. 459 and 460 does not destroy this conclusion. The subsequent equivalence with a continuous birational group fails in the reverse direction.

20. **Beauville repairs the general-type Rosenblatt–Comessatti assertion** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-8`.] (p. 462; §8). His appendix to Debarre (1982), pp. 343–346, proves `pg≥2q−4`, with equality exactly for a product of a genus-two curve and a curve of genus at least two. The source's reverse inequality thus forces equality. The product calculation gives `pg=2g,q=g+2,χ=g−1,K²=8(g−1)`. The equality argument uses positivity for a genus-g fibration over a genus-b curve, `χ≥(b−1)(g−1)≥q−3`, and the product criterion in the equality case. The original Rosenblatt and Comessatti papers were not independently read; Beauville explicitly identifies the historical gaps, and his later proof was read directly.

21. **The final bicanonical conclusion is true, but dimension alone does not prove it** [Commentary: `cp2-xi-9`.] (pp. 463–464; §9). Vanishing and Riemann–Roch give `P2−1=pa+p^(1)−1`; having at least four sections does not exclude a pencil. Xiao (1985), Theorem 1, gives the exact repair: the bicanonical map is generically finite except for `pg=q=0,K²=1`. The book explicitly allows a multiple presentation, so XI201 has been corrected to avoid attributing an unclaimed birational embedding. For K²=1, Debarre's bound proves regularity. For K²=2 there are pg=q=1 double covers of E^(2), with branch `6D0−2G0`, as stated in Lewis–Lyons (2018), Theorem 2.1. The canonical threshold pg>3 still does not by itself control image dimension or degree.
